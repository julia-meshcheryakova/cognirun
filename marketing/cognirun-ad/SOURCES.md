# Sources, claims and assets

## Historical routines (inspiration, not causal evidence or endorsements)

- Charles Darwin's daily Sandwalk: Leonard Darwin's first-hand recollection, https://darwin-online.org.uk/converted/Ancillary/1929_Leonard_A224.html. English Heritage's description: https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/home-of-charles-darwin-down-house/history/description/. The advert says daily walks, not that walking caused his scientific achievement. It does not attribute the phrase “thinking path” as a verified Darwin quotation.
- Ernest Hemingway writing while standing: George Plimpton, original Paris Review interview, Spring 1958, https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/4825/the-art-of-fiction-no-21-ernest-hemingway. This is posture, not proof of a performance increase.
- Haruki Murakami's running alongside writing: his own 2008 account, https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2008/06/09/haruki-murakami-the-running-novelist. He distinguishes running from thinking about writing in https://www.newyorker.com/culture/the-new-yorker-interview/the-underground-worlds-of-haruki-murakami. The advert does not claim that he composed novels while running.

## Walking experiment

Oppezzo, M., & Schwartz, D. L. (2014). Give your ideas some legs: The positive effect of walking on creative thinking. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition, 40(4), 1142–1152. DOI: 10.1037/a0036577.

Primary paper: https://aaalab.stanford.edu/assets/papers/2014/Give_your_ideas_some_legs.pdf

- The approximately 60% mean increase in creative output is reported in Experiment 1, which involved 48 undergraduate students. It refers to appropriate, novel alternate uses—not IQ, general intelligence, or all thinking.
- The same experiment reported a modest decline in compound remote associates (convergent-thinking) accuracy. The ad calls this a one-answer thinking task.
- Experiment 1 was seated then treadmill walking, rather than a fully counterbalanced order. Later experiments tested order and outdoor generalisation.
- No claim is made that all people, tasks or intensities benefit. This result is not a validation of CogniRun.

## Original visual asset

File: `public/images/runner-hero.png`.

Created using the built-in image-generation tool. Anonymous fictional adult runner; no stock-photo or celebrity likeness was requested. The full-resolution generated asset is included at the project path above.

Generation prompt:

> Use case: ads-marketing. Asset type: cinematic background for a 16:9 premium sports-technology motion advert for CogniRun, an exploratory brain-and-body running app. Create an original high-quality editorial photograph, very wide landscape composition. An anonymous adult female recreational runner in understated black training clothes and shoes with small acid-lime accents is jogging on a dark outdoor athletics track at blue hour. Place her fully in the RIGHTMOST THIRD of the image, full body visible, side-three-quarter view, natural believable anatomy and relaxed running form. The LEFT TWO THIRDS are moody near-black negative space with subtle curved running-track lane lines leading toward the distance, intentionally clear for large animated typography to be added later. Faint lavender floodlight haze and acid-lime rim light, deep charcoal blacks, tactile real rubber track, refined premium sports-campaign photography, cinematic 35mm grain, elegant dramatic contrast, realistic—not a superhero or science-fiction scene. Frame sufficiently wide for a later 9:16 crop focused on the runner on the right. No text, no numbers, no logos, no watermark, no visible brand names, no medical imagery, no charts, no glowing brain. This is conceptual campaign artwork, not documentary photography.

All other graphics are original frame-driven SVG/HTML typography and diagrams, or a functional untracked QR encoding of the public app URL. No article photographs or publisher charts are reproduced.
